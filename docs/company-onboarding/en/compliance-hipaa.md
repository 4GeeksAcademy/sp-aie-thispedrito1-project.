# Compliance guidelines — HIPAA (United States)

## Why this stage comes first
You will not receive access to any system that contains patient information until you pass this stage and Compliance approves it. There are no shortcuts: if you need more time, take it.

## What HIPAA protects
HIPAA protects protected health information (PHI): any health information that can identify a patient, such as a name together with a diagnosis, an appointment date or an insurance number. PHI exists in our record systems, billing platforms and patient messaging tools.

## The minimum necessary principle
When you use or share PHI, use only the minimum necessary to do the task. If a report works with counts, do not export patient-level rows. If you need one field, do not open the whole record. Having technical access to data does not mean you are allowed to look at it.

## Where PHI may and may not go
PHI may only live in systems that Compliance has approved and that are covered by a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with the vendor. PHI must never be pasted into Slack, Jira, Notion, personal email or any AI tool that is not approved.

## Access logging
Every access to a system with PHI is recorded in an access log, including who accessed which record and when. Compliance reviews these logs. Use your own account only: never share credentials.

## Incident notification
If you suspect that PHI was accessed, sent or exposed without authorisation, report it to Compliance the same day you notice it. Do not try to investigate on your own. Under HIPAA, affected individuals must be notified without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after the breach is discovered. That clock starts when the breach is discovered, so reporting it internally on time matters.
